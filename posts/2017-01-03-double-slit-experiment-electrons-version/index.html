Double-slit experiment
Electrons sent through two slits land on a screen in an interference pattern, as waves would; any measurement that tells which slit each electron went through makes the pattern disappear.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-slit_experiment

In modern physics, the double-slit experiment demonstrates that light and matter can exhibit behavior associated with both classical particles and classical waves.

In 1927, Davisson and Germer and, independently, George Paget Thomson and his research student Alexander Reid demonstrated that electrons show the same behavior, which was later extended to atoms and molecules.

With Electrons
“Double slit experiment with electrons”, a Physics Forums thread (April 2013), today titled “What does observing electrons mean in the double slit experiment”:
https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/what-does-observing-electrons-mean-in-the-double-slit-experiment.684465/1
The question (kjamha, 2013-04-09):

What does it mean to “look at the electrons”? Would it be more accurate to say “if the electrons interact with photons”? Or am I missing something?

The first answer (phinds, 2013-04-09):

ANY interaction that would allow knowledge of which slit the electron went through destroys the interference and basically forces the electron to act like a particle instead of a wave.
