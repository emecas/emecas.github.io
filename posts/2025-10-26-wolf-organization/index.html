Wolf Organization
https://share.google/aimode/4fmLADMoVfLIFMBpx

There are many documentaries that focus on wolf organizations and conservation efforts, including those covering reintroduction programs and advocacy groups. Several notable films include:

On specific organizations
Welcome Home (2024): This documentary from Reel Earth Films celebrates the reintroduction of wolves to Colorado after a public vote. It covers the story of the unique conservation success, featuring interviews with biologists, ranchers, and Native Americans.

Operation Wolf Patrol (2021): Eco-activist Rod Coronado, a Native American, is the subject of this film. He documents his fight to protect gray wolves being hunted in the U.S. by following and confronting hunters through his organization, Wolf Patrol.

Living with Wolves films: Before founding the non-profit Living with Wolves, filmmakers Jim and Jamie Dutcher produced several documentaries. Their 1994 Emmy-winning film, Wolf: Return of A Legend, helped build public support for wolf recovery efforts in Yellowstone and Central Idaho.

APEX Protection Project film: Independent filmmaker Evan Morton made a film about this non-profit, which is dedicated to rescuing captive-born wolves and wolfdogs that have been exploited.

On wolf reintroduction and coexistence
Wolf Land (2025): This documentary, which premiered on PBS, explores the complex relationship between wolves and humans in northeastern Washington. It features a “wolf-protecting cowboy” and a cattle rancher trying to find common ground.

The Trouble With Wolves (2019): This film examines the complexities of coexistence with wolves in the American West following their reintroduction to Yellowstone. It includes balanced perspectives from biologists, ranchers, hunters, and wolf advocates.

The Wolf That Changed America (2008): This episode of the PBS series Nature tells the story of Ernest Thompson Seton, a bounty hunter whose encounter with a wolf led him to advocate for the establishment of national parks.

In the Valley of the Wolves (2008): Featured on the PBS series Nature, this film follows the most famous wolf pack in America, the Druid wolves of Yellowstone’s Lamar Valley, as they face challenges and competition.

On specific species and issues
Resilience: Story of the American Red Wolf (2024): This film highlights the efforts of conservationists and organizations working to save the critically endangered red wolf from extinction.

The Right to be Wild (2024): Filmmaker Madeleine Torneman documents the story of the Mexican gray wolf’s near-extinction and return to the Southwest, emphasizing the work of those involved in its recovery.

Death of a Legend (1971): Produced by the National Film Board of Canada, this documentary by Bill Mason was an early attempt to dispel negative myths about wolves by portraying the animal’s natural life and pack structure.
